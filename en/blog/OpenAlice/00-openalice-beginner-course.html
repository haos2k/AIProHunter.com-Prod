OpenAlice Beginner's Course: Build Your One-Person AI Trading Studio in 5 Articles

Apr 15, 2026·4 min read

Most people's image of "AI trading" looks something like this: a mysterious black box, a pile of indicators nobody understands, and one day your account goes to zero — if you're not a quant engineer by trade, it feels like you're not even allowed to play.

OpenAlice is built to break down exactly that barrier.

It's an open-source AI trading agent that integrates research, analysis, order execution, and automated monitoring into a single conversational interface. You don't need to write Python strategies, spin up a trading server, or juggle ten monitoring windows at once — you just talk to the AI, and let it do the work.

This five-article series takes OpenAlice apart piece by piece and walks you through it, one stop at a time.

What Is OpenAlice

OpenAlice describes itself as "a one-person Wall Street." That's not a marketing line — it's an accurate description of what it's designed to do:

  • Research — Cross-asset market data (stocks, crypto, commodities, forex, macroeconomics), all accessible through a single interface
  • Trading — Multiple brokerage accounts consolidated into a single Unified Trading Account (UTA); the AI only interacts with the UTA, never directly with broker APIs
  • Automation — Cron schedules, Heartbeat market monitoring, Webhook triggers, all running on the same event log
  • Memory — The Brain system gives the AI persistent working memory across conversations, so you don't have to re-explain context every time

In short: what used to require a three-person analyst team, OpenAlice tries to let one person handle alone.

  • Engineers or developers who want to explore AI trading but don't know where to start
  • Traders who are already monitoring manually and want to automate parts of their workflow
  • Anyone curious about the intersection of "AI Agents + financial markets" who wants to understand before diving in
  • Anyone who thinks "quantitative trading seems hard" but just needs a good entry point
