What the Directory Structure Looks Like

openspec/
├── specs/              # What your system looks like right now (source of truth)
│   └── <domain>/
│       └── spec.md
├── changes/            # Each pending change (one folder per change)
│   └── <change-name>/
│       ├── proposal.md
│       ├── design.md
│       ├── tasks.md
│       └── specs/      # Delta spec for this change
│           └── <domain>/
│               └── spec.md
└── config.yaml         # Project configuration (optional)

Two core directories:

specs/ is your system's current documentation — it describes how the system behaves today, organized into domain-based folders. You can organize by feature area (auth/, payments/), by component (api/, frontend/), or by business boundary — whatever matches how your project is structured.

changes/ holds in-progress modifications — every feature you want to add or modify lives here in its own folder, containing its proposal, design, task list, and delta spec. Once done, you archive it and the delta spec merges back into specs/.

Start Telling Your AI to Use OpenSpec

Once initialized, open your AI assistant (Cursor, Claude Code, Windsurf, etc.) and type:

/opsx:propose <what you want to do>

For example:

/opsx:propose add-dark-mode

The AI will start building the change folder and related artifacts for you.

Not sure what /opsx:propose does? The next article explains the core concepts first, then we'll walk through the full workflow.
