OpenSpec Beginner's Guide: 5 Articles to Master Spec Management for AI Development

Mar 31, 2026·4 min read

A lot of people use AI to write code like this: type "add this feature," wait for the agent to generate a pile of code that looks fine, then discover when it runs that everything went sideways — three revisions and still stuck in the same place. The problem isn't that the AI isn't smart enough. It's that nobody clearly explained what needed to be done. OpenSpec is built to solve exactly that.

Why This Series Is Worth Reading

OpenSpec isn't another spec-writing format. It's more of a working rhythm: clarify what to build first, then let AI get to work. Sounds simple — and yet most people skip this step, then pay triple the cost during the revision phase. This series breaks the whole process into five articles, from "why do it this way" to "how to actually run through it," each focused on one topic, leaving you with a reusable playbook.

Who This Series Is For

  • People who regularly ask AI to write code but get inconsistent results that need many revisions
  • People who already know they "should write requirements," but don't know how to make AI follow them
  • People who want to run AI-assisted development more smoothly in personal projects or small teams

Series Reading Map

The route goes: understand the pain → set up the tools → grasp the core concepts → complete a full workflow → see how it works in real scenarios. Five articles, five stops. Reading in order is recommended, though each article can also be read on its own.

What You'll Learn in 5 Articles

  1. Why AI Development Without Specs Always Ends Up Costing More
    Preview: Starting from the familiar frustration of "AI works hard but results are unpredictable," this explains what problem OpenSpec solves and why its design is "fluid rather than rigid." After reading, you'll know whether this path is worth taking.

  2. OpenSpec Installation and Initialization: 5 Minutes to Get Your Project Spec-Ready
    Preview: Install, initialize, and understand the directory structure. This article is pure action — once done, your project will have OpenSpec up and running.

  3. specs, changes, artifacts: OpenSpec's Three Core Concepts
    Preview: Understand what each of these three things is and why they're kept separate. Without understanding this article, you'll use the commands but won't understand why they work the way they do.

  4. /opsx:propose → apply → archive: Running a Complete OpenSpec Workflow
    Preview: Walks you through the complete flow from "I have an idea" to "implementation done, spec archived," explaining exactly what each command does.

  5. Real-World Usage: New Features, Bug Fixes, Adding OpenSpec to Existing Projects
    Preview: Maps what you learned in the first four articles to everyday tasks — new features, bug fixes, and adding OpenSpec to legacy projects each have their own corresponding playbook.

Recommended Reading Order

Read in order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5. Article one builds motivation, article two sets up the tools, article three establishes concepts, article four walks through a full cycle, article five maps to real work. If time is short, at minimum read 1 and 4 — that covers the core "why" and "how"; add 3 later to fill in the conceptual gaps.

Quick-Start Version

One sentence: When adding or changing a feature, start with /opsx:propose, let AI generate the spec + design + task list, confirm it looks right, then /opsx:apply to implement, and /opsx:archive when done. Don't skip this workflow — the chance of AI going off the rails drops dramatically. The details are in the five articles above. This article's job: know the map, plan the route, get going.
