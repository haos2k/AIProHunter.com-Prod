Superpowers Beginner Course: 5 Articles to Master Skills, Workflows, and How AI Agents Work

Mar 29, 2026·4 min read

This is the Superpowers series homepage: rather than cramming all the knowledge details into one post, it helps you get oriented, establish a reading order, and jump directly to the right article. Think of an AI Agent as an incredibly driven and powerful AI development team — but only if you tell it "which track to run on" first. Otherwise, the engine room fills up fast with enthusiasm and chaos.

Why This Series Is Worth Reading

Superpowers isn't about piling on more messy code — it's about locking in a working rhythm: understand and design first, then plan and implement, then verify. Most beginner pain points aren't about the model being unintelligent; they're about jumping straight to writing code, skipping the clarification step, and ending up going in circles after three rounds of revisions. This series breaks "which skill to call first, and when to pause and think about design" into five shorter articles. By the end, you'll have a repeatable playbook — not just scattered tips.

Who This Series Is For

  • People just starting to use Agents and Skills, who've heard of Superpowers but don't know where to begin
  • People who already know how to give instructions but often feel the agent is "trying hard but going off-track," and want to add workflow and quality thinking
  • People who want to clearly separate spec / plan / implementation / verification and stop reinventing the wheel every time

If you just want a "one-liner shortcut," jump straight to the Quick Start section at the end; for a more solid foundation, read all five articles in the recommended order.

Series Reading Map

The full path looks like this: understand the rules and worldview first → learn "why you need to design before starting" → then understand how spec, plan, and implementation connect → add three quality guardrails → finally apply it all to real work scenarios (new features, bug fixes, wrap-ups). Each article below is one stop on this map — click in for that stop's key lessons.

What You'll Learn in 5 Articles

  1. What Is Superpowers? Understanding the Core Rules of the Skills Workflow
    Intro: Build a shared vocabulary — what problem Superpowers solves, which rules are non-negotiable. After reading this, you'll understand why the rest of the series is structured the way it is.

  2. using-superpowers and brainstorming: Why You Can't Skip Design and Jump Straight to Building
    Intro: Turns "think it through before acting" into executable steps. This article plugs the most common pitfall — starting implementation before requirements are clear.

  3. What's the Difference Between spec, plan, and implementation? The Right Workflow for Superpowers
    Intro: Clarifies the output and boundaries of three stages, so the agent follows them in order instead of blurring spec, plan, and code into one messy blob.

  4. TDD, systematic-debugging, verification-before-completion: Three Quality Guardrails for AI Agents
    Intro: Adds testing, debugging, and verification steps before and after implementation — reducing the risk of "looks done but isn't."

  5. New Features, Bug Fixes, Wrap-Ups: Superpowers in Real-World Workflows
    Intro: Maps the learned workflows to everyday task types, so you know which skill to start with and in what order for today's ticket.

Recommended Reading Order

Read in order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5. Lay the rules and worldview first, then explore pre-work brainstorming, then the spec / plan / implementation relay, then quality guardrails, and finally use real-world workflows to tie it all together. If you're truly short on time, read at least 1 and 3, then 4 as needed; come back to 2 and 5 when you can — the lessons will stick better.

Quick Start

One sentence: If there's a matching process skill, follow the workflow first; then let implementation enter; and be able to say what you verified when you're done.
If you can only remember three keywords, remember brainstorming (clarify), writing-plans (plan), verification-before-completion (verify) — corresponding to "don't rush in blindly," "don't change things randomly," "don't pretend it's done." The details and timing are in the five articles above. This article's job ends here: get oriented, set the schedule, and go.
