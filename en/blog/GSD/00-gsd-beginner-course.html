GSD Beginner Course: 6 Articles to Help You Actually Get Things Done with AI

Apr 16, 2026·4 min read

Many people's first reaction after adopting AI is "things feel faster" — then three months later they look back and realize they were just spinning their wheels more efficiently. Plans got more polished, Notion got more organized, chat histories piled up thicker, but the actual output shipped? Not obviously more.

This series is about: why, and how to fix it.

What Problem This Series Solves

GSD — Get Shit Done — literally means "finish what you started." This isn't some high-end management system. It answers one very practical question: Did you actually move the important things forward today?

That alone is where most people get stuck.

Not because they aren't trying — but because there's a gap between "effort" and "completion" that almost nobody talks about clearly. AI tools let you research faster, draft more fluently, and plan more easily. But if your execution mindset hasn't kept up, AI just makes you more efficient at going in circles.

These six articles dismantle that gap, piece by piece.

Who This Series Is For

  • People who've written tons of AI-assisted notes and plans, but whose completion rate quietly embarrasses them
  • People who feel like they have no shortage of tools or ideas, but always get stuck at the "actually doing it" step
  • People who want to upgrade AI from a "chat toy" to something that actually ships work — but can't figure out what's missing
  • Solo developers, creators, and anyone who needs to push things from zero to done on their own

If you just want the core one-liner, jump to the Quick Start at the end. If you want to understand the full logic, read all six in order.

Series Reading Map

These six articles start from "diagnosing the problem" and move toward "building a routine." Each stands alone, but together they form a complete execution framework:

1. Why Can't You Finish Things Even with AI? A GSD Problem Diagnosis
State the problem clearly — it's not that you're lazy or the AI isn't good enough. There are a few very specific execution breakpoints, and being stuck at those means no tool will save you.

2. Done > Perfect: The Core GSD Mindset, from Planning Paralysis to Actually Moving
"Perfectionism" sounds like a virtue, but at the execution level it's nearly always fatal. This article is about resetting the threshold for "good enough to ship" in your head.

3. Breaking Down Tasks with AI: From Vague Requirements to an Actionable List
Vagueness is the number one killer of completion rates. This article shows you how to use AI to turn "I want to do something" into a concrete task list you can start today — not a longer to-do list, but clearer next steps.

4. The Execution Loop: Designing Rhythm to Keep Your AI Agent on Track
AI is great at charging ahead, but you have to set the direction. This article covers designing a "start → execute → close → iterate" loop that keeps the tool running on the right track, so you're not recalibrating from scratch every time.

5. The 3 GSD Traps: Why More Planning Leads to Less Completion
There are a few very common patterns of "looks like hard work but nothing actually got done" — this article names them so you can recognize where you're getting stuck.

6. Real-World GSD: Practical Playbooks for Code, Writing, and Proposals
Map everything learned to concrete task types, so you know what rhythm to open with and how to close out today's specific work.

Suggested Reading Order

Read in order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6. Start by diagnosing the root cause, build the core mindset, then move to task breakdown and rhythm design, identify common traps, and finally run the whole system through real-world scenarios.

If time is short, read at least 1 and 3: know where the problem is, know how to turn vagueness into clarity — those two things alone will meaningfully improve your completion rate.

Quick Start

One sentence: Define "the next concrete step you can take right now" clearly, then take it — don't wait for the plan to be complete.

If you can only remember three keywords: Break down (turn tasks into concrete actions), Start (don't wait for perfect), Ship (get it out the door, then iterate). The detail and rhythm — that's what the six articles above are for. This article's job ends here: know the map, set the schedule, get moving.
